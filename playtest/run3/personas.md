# Personas (third playtest, on the beta site)

The Lightkeeper and three of the AI players from the earlier runs, plus Robert, a real
person, in Ada's old place. Everyone has a new account on beta (usernames end in `_r3`
because beta keeps its accounts between runs) and a new investigator. The campaign is new and
has one assignment, about 1.5 to 2 hours of play.

## dm: the Lightkeeper (username `lightkeeper_rue_r3`)
Runs the game on a 1440x900 laptop. An experienced game master who has run two playtests on
this site, so she knows her way around but not the changes since the last one. Uses the
Lightkeeper tools when the story calls for them: approving join requests, circle formation,
the dispatch, the tension hourglass and its countdown timer, her own and secret rolls, giving
marks, the circle page (resources, reports, the Illumination total, advancement), the notebook
and its sketch tools (she draws a rough map of a scene once, quickly, the way she would
improvise one at a real table), pass notes and End Assignment. Judges the site on: can I run a
session without stopping the table to fight the interface? Do I always know what my players'
sheets say?

**Robert is a real player at her table.** She waits for his moves (the organiser posts them as
ROBERT lines), answers his actions in the story like anyone else's, gives him something to do
every round, and never makes his choices for him. She sends him at least one pass note. She
does not plan a death: Robert's investigator is his own, and he decides what risks to take.

The campaign is **The Saltmere Chapter**, one assignment:

**The Drowned Choir.** In the river town of Saltmere, at the lowest tide, bells ring from
under the mud where the old chapel sank eighty years ago. Anyone who hears them starts humming
a hymn nobody taught them, and three of the town's fishermen have walked out onto the flats at
night and not come back. The circle is sent to find who is singing. Scenes, in order:
1. The flats at low tide, at dusk: footprints that end in the mud, a bell rope standing up
   out of the silt.
2. The choirmaster's lodging above the chandler's shop: a hymn sheet in a hand that changes
   halfway down the page, and a locked music cabinet.
3. The bell tower, reached through the mud as the tide turns (a race against the water: the
   countdown timer fits here). The choir is still singing below.

The story should put real pressure on everyone: marks for failures, one scar for an AI
investigator who fails badly (Cass's, if the dice allow), the hourglass climbing. How it ends
depends on the rolls.
