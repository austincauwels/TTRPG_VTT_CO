# The Drowned Bell: an AI playtest of Candela VTT

## What this is

Candela VTT is a web app, built by Austin, for running the tabletop role-playing game Candela Obscura (Darrington Press) online. It handles the game's rules: dice pools, gilded dice, drives and resistances, marks and scars, the circle's resources, the Illumination track, advancement, and a shared notebook. Everyone's screen updates live.

On the evening of 8 October 2026 (Pacific time), five AI agents played a full session of Candela Obscura on a private copy of the site. One ran the game as the Lightkeeper (the game master), and four were players. Each had its own web browser, its own account and its own device size, and each played through the real interface, clicking and tapping like a person would. They also had a shared channel for table talk: the narration, the in-character lines and the out-of-character questions you would hear around a real table. That talk is the transcript below. The site's own activity log (rolls, notes, marks) is mixed in, in time order.

The point was to find what breaks, what confuses people and what could be better, so every agent kept notes as it played. By the end they had logged 277 findings. These were grouped into 122 distinct problems and ideas, and 113 of them were re-tested independently on a fresh copy of the site, with the cause found in the code: 96 were confirmed and 16 partly confirmed. The eight most serious then faced a second check that tried to knock them down, and all eight stood.

The session ran for about three and a half hours, from about 5:00 PM to 8:30 PM Pacific.

## The table

- **Ada Quill**, on a big 1920 by 1080 desktop screen, plays **Dr. Imogen Thale**, a Scholar (Doctor). Ada is a veteran and a rules lawyer who checks every number against the rulebook.
- **Bram Okafor**, on a phone, plays **Jonah Hale**, a Muscle (Soldier) and ex-sergeant. Bram is new to tabletop games and plays from the sofa.

- **Dev Lindqvist**, on a tablet, plays **Edda Rook**, a Weird (Occultist). Dev is a roleplayer and note-keeper who lives in the shared notebook.

Together their investigators formed a circle called **The Ninth Night**.

## The story in brief

In the fog-bound harbour town of Saint Aldric, a church bell that sank with its tower forty years ago has started to ring under the water at night. Each time it rings, someone in town forgets a person they loved, completely.

The circle arrived at the harbour office at dusk. Harbourmaster Wendell Pike showed them a crew photograph with a gap where a man used to stand. He could not remember the partner who had worked beside him for thirty years. Dr. Thale examined Pike and the photograph and drew out details: the missing man had lost the top of his ring finger to a winch. Edda found a second handwriting fading out of the tide ledger, ending three weeks ago with "Taking the skiff out to look. Back by ten." Latch picked the padlock on a nameless eighth locker and found the missing man's oilskin, his boots, a wedding ring and a photograph of his widow, Nell. His name was Tam Carrow. Then the bell rang early, under the water, and something noticed them.

In the drowned church's flooding crypt, with a countdown running on everyone's screen, the circle learned the truth. A drowned congregation pulls the bell rope through Tam's hand, and every name the bell reads becomes a soul for its pews. They escaped the rising water on Latch's best roll, at a cost: the Doctor's shin was split, Jonah was half drowned, and Latch's wrist was ringed red.

At the top of the tower, at the turn of the tide, with the hourglass run empty, Edda took the bell's fourth ring through her own body. Speaking in the voice of the drowned curate, Parson Absalom Grey, she forbade the banns. The congregation let go of the rope. Jonah, who never let go, hauled the bell's tongue out of the belfry, and the bell now swings in silence. The price was Tam Carrow: his name stays read, and Saint Aldric will never remember him. Nell still sets two places at her table. Only the circle remembers him. By dawn the fog had lifted.

Afterwards the circle filled its Illumination track, advanced (gaining the ability Stamina Training), and each investigator took advancements.

## What the playtesters found

277 findings: 42 bugs, 2 rules problems, 153 notes on confusing or awkward moments, 29 ideas, and 51 things that work well and should be kept. After merging duplicates and re-testing, the verified report lists 44 bugs and rules problems (4 of them high severity), 67 confusing or awkward spots, 10 ideas, and 11 things to keep.

### What to fix first

The verified report ends with a priority list. In plain words:

1. When a player reconnects, give back any gilded roll still waiting for a choice, so a reload can't erase it.
2. Give every roll an ID so the server never counts it twice, and show clearly when the connection has dropped.
3. Make the assignment report show what was filed, keep it across tab switches, and refuse silent overwrites.
4. Guard circle resources against double-clicks, with a short Undo, and let the Lightkeeper give a spend back.
5. Add up the Illumination on the Lightkeeper's report page using the rulebook's scoring.
6. Keep a short activity log history on the server and replay it whenever a screen reconnects.
7. Log every Lightkeeper change and put a "new" dot on the phone menu until the player has looked.
8. Keep the hourglass, timer and newest log lines in view on phones, tablets and every section of the desk.
9. Never let something that is waiting for a player disappear: advancement picks, Behind Me offers, an approval.
10. Make the dice say what they will do: the real dice count, what each gilded choice means, and no stale reroll offers.

### The most serious problems

1. **Reloading during a gilded dice choice gives a free reroll.** The roll vanishes for everyone, the drive points spent on it are lost, and the next roll silently replaces it.
2. **A roll made during a brief connection hiccup can count twice.** The screen says the roll was not thrown, so the player rolls again, but the first roll still reaches the table.
3. **The assignment report forgets it was sent.** The ticks vanish on Send and the form comes back blank, so sending again silently replaces the first report.
4. **Double-clicking a circle resource spends it twice,** using up both of a player's between-assignment spends at once.
5. **The activity log is not saved.** A reload, a second tab or a trip back to the hub wipes every roll and note from that screen. (The second check rated this one medium rather than high.)

### Places where the site and the rulebook disagree

- **Patch Up, the Doctor's healing ability, is offered after every Focus roll,** so one investigation roll also heals an ally.
- **Marks the Lightkeeper sets by hand skip the reactions they should trigger,** such as an ally's Behind Me or the Weird's Let Them In.
- **The drive spinner promises more dice than the game allows.** It shows five extra dice, then rolls four, because no roll can have more than six dice.
- **Nothing adds up the Illumination from the assignment reports,** and the rulebook's scoring for keys is missing. The circle's three Illumination questions appear on every player's report, so one answer can be ticked four times. In this session the circle advanced about five Illumination early.

### Problems several players hit

- **A waiting player never sees their approval** until they press "Check again".
- **Relationship answers get garbled.** The relationship question is pasted into the answer box, so a player's answer ends up spliced into the middle of it.
- **Choosing "Later" on the advancement screen leaves no way back to your picks** without reloading the page.
- **Voting on the circle's name is unreliable.** The names re-sort while you click, so a vote can land on the wrong name, and the same name in different capital letters splits the vote.
- **Sketches and photos in the notebook never reach the other players' screens.**

### The biggest themes for how it feels

- **Lightkeeper changes arrive without anything on screen to say so.** The site plays a tick when tension rises and a chime when the timer runs out, but a new dispatch, a private note or open reports show up with no highlight, badge or log line. All four players noticed.
- **On phones and tablets, the hourglass and timer are never near the dice.** On smaller laptops, parts of the desk sit out of view.
- **Passing notes is confusing.** There is no way to send a note to just the Lightkeeper, you are offered yourself as a recipient, and "private" notes don't say the Lightkeeper can read them.
- **Game terms go unexplained,** and hover tooltips never appear on a touch screen.

### What works well and should be kept

- **The desk stays current on its own.** Rolls, marks, the hourglass, the timer and the Lightkeeper's changes reached every screen live, without reloading, all session long. Three players rolling in the same millisecond showed up identically, in the same order, on all five screens.
- **The dice follow the rulebook to the letter.** Pool sizes, zero-rated rolls, gilded choices, criticals, burning resistance and drive costs all checked out.
- **The character creator works well,** including on a phone and a tablet: the path cards, the checklist of what is left, portrait uploads and handwriting fonts.
- **The countdown timer agreed across all five screens** within a few hundredths of a second.
- **Sealing the circle's charter carried everything over correctly,** and circle advancement follows the book.
- **The notebook feels like paper:** handwriting fonts, photos shown as taped prints, and sketches that stay editable.
- **Joining shows up live on the Lightkeeper's desk,** and each approval drops a polaroid onto it.

### In the players' own words

- **The Lightkeeper:** what slowed the table most was the loop of calling for a roll, reading it and giving marks, which the site only half carries. There is no at-a-glance view of the whole circle, so she opened four sheets one at a time. Keep exactly as it is: her copy of every sheet stays current on its own.
- **Ada (desktop):** the rules engine is right but silent. A one-line "rules receipt" on each roll would have answered most of her questions. Keep the live desk and the by-the-book dice.
- **Bram (phone):** what got in the way most was never knowing something had changed. One "new" dot on the menu would fix most of it, and he'd like to tap any game word for a two-line explanation. Keep the sheet-as-dice-tray and the advancement screen.
- **Cass (laptop):** the desk's memory lives in the browser tab, so a reload, a second tab or a stray Escape each lose something. Every one-click action should get the same double-click guard the dice already have. Keep the server as referee and the honest connection warnings.
- **Dev (tablet):** the notebook is cut off from the table. Let it sit beside the log and sync its edits. Keep the notebook's paper feel and the two-way relationships.
